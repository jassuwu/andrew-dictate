# andrew dictate — v1 spec

> hold a key, talk, get text.
> free forever. fully local. small enough to read.

decisions in this spec are backed by ADRs kept in `docs/adr/` and research in `~/repos/personal/andrew-dictate-research/` — **both are local-only and deliberately untracked** (see `.gitignore`), a private archive of how each decision was reached, including the ones later reversed. if you cloned this repo, you have the spec, not the archive; nothing here depends on reading it. terms are defined in `GLOSSARY.md` and used exactly.

## 1. product

- **name:** Andrew Dictate. binary/app: `Andrew Dictate.app`, cask `andrew-dictate`, repo `jassuwu/andrew-dictate` (MIT, public day one — ADR 0010).
- **platform:** macOS 26+, Apple Silicon only. *(was 14+ until 0.8.0; the glass windows and the meeting tap both need 26, and a floor that moves once is better than two features that each say "not on this mac".)*
- **thesis:** frontier-fast dictation, with the smallest possible surface: no account, no cloud, no settings maze, no subscription. trust is architectural. the app goes online in three places and no others: the model download, the about window's `check for updates`, and the daily update check, which sends dictate.jass.gg the running version and nothing else (ADR 0043).
- **non-goals (v1):** windows/linux, iOS, always-on listening (deferred — ADR 0003), ~~meeting transcription~~ (**crossed 2026-08-22 — ADR 0023**: manual start and stop. since ADR 0047 the app watches which apps hold the mic so it can ask, and never acts on what it sees), ~~history browser~~ (**superseded — ADR 0022**), App Store.
- **voice command mode: removed (2026-08-06).** shipped in early v1 (router tiers, agent delegation, ask/screen-ask), cut entirely to focus the product on dictation. this spec describes the app as it is; command-mode sections and terms are gone from here and the glossary.

## 2. the pipeline

one pipeline, one sink (ADR 0003):

```
hold fn ──▶ mic capture ──▶ key-up ──▶ engine (parakeet v2, prewarmed)
                                              │ transcript
                                              ▼ deterministic cleaner (always)
                                              ▼ inserter (paste into frontmost app)
```

- **engine:** parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2 int8 via FluidAudio, batch on key-up, prewarmed at launch with one dummy inference (ADR 0002, 0007). v3 optional in settings. engine sits behind an `Engine` protocol (streaming engines are additive later). **nothing waits on it forever:** a take gets four seconds, or a quarter of its length once that is longer. a take the engine misses ends `couldn't transcribe — tap to try again` with the samples kept for a tap, and whatever the engine says later goes nowhere. the app then asks the engine a second of silence off the main thread and reloads the model in the background if that goes unanswered too. a second missed take in a row says `speech model isn't responding — restarting it` and reloads it. a reload that fails, or is still going after a minute, is retried by the next press, out loud. **awake by key-up:** a press wakes the engine with half a second of silence while you talk, unless it ran in the last ten seconds — the neural engine idles down between dictations, and a take handed to it cold waits on it. a take never runs beside a wake still in flight; it waits for it. at key-up the engine is asked first, and the chime, the lamp, the target and the clipboard snapshot all happen while it works.
- **capture:** AVAudioEngine, hardware-native format, graph prepared at launch. **capture is disposable:** each one owns one engine and one serial queue, and the main thread never touches the engine — it awaits, and gives up after ~1.5 s (`microphone isn't responding`). a fresh engine opens the system default input by name. a default input or output change, a device list change, a wake or a display change makes it stale; the next press gets a fresh one, and after ~500 ms of quiet the stale one is thrown away and a fresh one built ready — engine made, default mic bound, graph prepared, no i/o started — so the first press after a monitor or the lid starts warm (with pre-roll on, it starts listening). nothing is ever rebuilt in place. the mic's first audio, which lights the lamp and plays the chime, is reported by a sink node called every i/o cycle; the take itself is captured by the tap. **pre-roll is a user toggle (ADR 0012), chosen at onboarding:** ON = a ~300ms rolling ring buffer runs while the app is active (mic stays open, indicator stays lit, buffer lives only in memory and is discarded continuously) so the first word is never clipped; OFF = mic starts at key-down, maximum privacy posture. changeable in settings.
- **cleaner (deterministic, always on — ADR 0004, 0019, 0020):** eight staged transforms — whitespace normalization, dictionary substitutions, spoken punctuation, email/url/number parsing, capitalization, punctuation finishing. **the dictionary runs before the parsers** so it reads the engine's own words: the word you point at in "fix a word…" is the word an entry matches, whether cleanup is on or off. **it renders what you said into how it is written, and never decides you meant something else** (ADR 0020): the three stages that edited rather than transcribed — self-corrections, repetition collapse, filler removal — are gone, because each of them could silently change correct speech into something that still read perfectly. stumbles reach the page. microseconds, no model, pure functions. not user-disableable: it is where the dictionary and spoken punctuation live, and without it you get raw asr. **a dictation aimed at Andrew Dictate's own window runs the dictionary only** — a correction is a word, not a sentence, so "cache" is not saved as "Cache."
- **hotkeys (ADR 0008):** fn = dictation, rebindable, chord-cancel semantics. NSEvent flagsChanged monitoring; no Input Monitoring permission. **locked recording:** double-tap the dictation key to lock its capture hands-free; a single tap ends it and inserts as normal. the whole gesture is written under `key` in settings › dictation — hold, double-tap to lock, one tap to end, esc to cancel — so the lock is read rather than stumbled into.

## 3. dictation

- key-up → transcript → cleaner → **inserter**.
- **insertion strategy (v1): transactional paste only.** snapshot pasteboard (all types) — read off the main thread while the engine works, and read again at the paste if anything was copied in between — write plain text, synthetic cmd-V resolved for the active layout, verify change, restore only if `changeCount` still ours. the transcript is marked `org.nspasteboard.TransientType` so clipboard managers do not keep a copy of it. AX selected-text insertion is v1.x.
- **target safety:** frontmost bundle id + focused-element captured at key-up (aiming at the field you want while still talking is the normal thing to do); re-verified before paste. focus changed → don't paste; transcript stays on the clipboard + HUD shows "copied — focus changed · ⌘V to paste." every copied-instead pill carries the verb: the panel cannot be clicked, so the recovery has to be something the hands already know.
- **secure fields:** detected via AX subrole → never auto-insert; HUD offers explicit copy. a dictation refused for a secure field is never written to `dictations.jsonl`, and its clipboard copy is marked `org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType` so clipboard managers do not record it.
- **what it keeps (ADR 0022, 0026):** every delivered dictation is written to `dictations.jsonl` in application support — raw + inserted text, time, engine, key-up→inserted — **on by default**, stated in onboarding, with the toggle and `delete all` in settings. cancelled dictations are not kept; there was no text. chmod 0600. this is a deliberate contrast with pre-roll's off-by-default: pre-roll opens a microphone, this keeps text already produced and already pasted.
- **ceiling:** capture stops itself at five minutes and keeps what it heard; the pill says `five minutes — that's the cap`. **esc** cancels anything in flight — nothing pasted, nothing kept.
- **learning from your fixes (ADR 0046):** after a delivered dictation the app watches the words it pasted, in that field, for a minute, and nothing else. each read is our words plus 40 characters either side; our words are found again by their first and last words, and when neither is there the dictation is given up on. the watch ends after 60 s, when focus leaves the field, when our words are gone, or at the next press. password fields and the app's own windows are never watched. a change counts only if it replaces one to three words we pasted with one to three that sound like them. added or deleted words, rewrites, case, punctuation, inflections like `large` → `larger` and swaps between common words like `there` → `their` are your writing, not a mishearing. fixes are counted in memory, one per dictation, and a restart forgets them. the second identical fix becomes a dictionary entry keyed on the engine's own words, and only if that entry gives back the text you fixed. it is marked `learned`. the pill says `learned: <word>` once — learned during a meeting or a take, it is said when the pill is free, even after the two minutes. the menu offers `undo learned: <word>` for two minutes, and the dictionary tab shows the mark beside a one-click remove. removing a learned entry from either place means it is never learned again; the pair is kept in `never-learn.json` beside the dictionary. a learned entry is an ordinary substitution and yours like any other. the cleaner's rules are untouched (ADR 0020).
- **escape hatch:** ~~"copy last" in the menu~~ **removed 2026-08-22 (ADR 0030).** it was justified as the way out when a paste fails — but a failed paste *already* leaves the text on the clipboard ("copied — secure field", "copied — focus changed"), so the hatch was covering a hole that was already covered. wanting it again is what the archive is for — so the archive row carries `copy`, which puts the inserted text (not the raw) on the clipboard and reads `copied` for 1.2 s. the raw transcript is still held, and still feeds "fix a word…" — which is the one action the menu keeps, because it is the only one that is time-sensitive.

## 4. hud

one nonactivating `NSPanel` (borderless, floating, all-spaces) that is never key and lets every click through, except over a pill with a button: an invisible stage, sized once per screen for the widest line of text, never morphing. everything visible is Liquid Glass inside it, in one container, so a change of state is a change of shape:

- **the tube (2026-09-22).** the lamp is a 6-point glass tube, 112 wide, drawn by hand in the canvas: a translucent tan body that takes the backdrop through it, a pale rim along the top, a shade along the bottom, a faint outline so it holds on white, a core that comes up with your voice, a blurred halo spilling by loudness — dim ember while prewarming for a key you already pressed (a launch-time warm-up shows nothing on screen and says `loading the speech model…` in the menu instead), the same ember from the press until the mic's first audio lands, then lit — the start chime plays then too, so the lit lamp and the chime both mean the mic is hearing you, not that a key went down — hot when you speak, then a cool-out that collapses it to a hot dot and fades. **success is silent: the afterglow is the whole goodbye.** it copies what a gold-tinted Liquid Glass ribbon looks like in a key window. it is not Liquid Glass, because Liquid Glass draws dimmed — no refraction, no highlight — in any window that is not key, and the panel must never be key: it would take the keyboard from the app you are dictating into. the bare gold line it replaces (2026-08-12) vanished over light pages; the glass capsule with eleven bars before that is not coming back.
- **the pill.** the exceptional-message shape, and the only thing that ever speaks: `copied — secure field · ⌘V to paste` · `copied — focus changed · ⌘V to paste` · `copied — couldn't paste it · ⌘V to paste` · `copied — what you said before the lock · ⌘V to paste` · `the clipboard is busy — nothing was copied` · `couldn't transcribe — tap to try again` · `heard nothing` · `still finishing the last one` · `recording was lost` · `speech model failed — retrying` · `speech model isn't responding — restarting it` · `speech model didn't download — finish setup` · `downloading the speech model — about 460 mb` · `downloading the speech model — 42%` · `loading the speech model…` · `microphone access is off` · `no microphone available` · `microphone isn't responding` · `no sound from AirPods Pro` · `the mic changed — pasted what i had.` · `thirty seconds left` · `five minutes — that's the cap. pasted what i had.`. every pill is also posted as a VoiceOver announcement — the failure rule holds for users who cannot look. dark glass with pale gold text, legible over a page. it shares the tube's stage, so the tube fades into it rather than the window changing shape.
- **a pill with a button (ADR 0047).** a question gets one button, set into the pill's own glass: a small capsule of the same glass, its word in the pill's pale gold a step heavier, quiet, never system blue. three questions have one: `call in zoom — record it?` with `record` for 15 s, `call ended — stop recording?` with `stop` for 30 s, and `still recording?` with `stop` for 30 s (§11). the button does what the menu item of the same name does, and nothing else on the hud starts or stops anything. a click on the pill beside the button is an answer too. its time stops once the pointer moves onto the pill, so it never leaves from under a hand on its way to the button. a pointer already resting where the pill came up stops nothing, and no hand holds a pill up for more than a minute in all. the panel takes the mouse only while the pointer is over such a pill, and it is never key, so a click on the button leaves the keyboard in the app you were typing in. a question waits its turn. it never goes up over a take, a sentence or setup, and pressing the dictation key takes the pill from it, unanswered. there is no key for the button; the menu offers the same actions, and that is the path for the keyboard and for VoiceOver. the button's accessibility label says the whole action (`record the call in zoom`), and the question is announced like every pill. a pill without a button is unchanged.
- **the meeting's light (2026-10-03).** a full-screen call hides the menu bar and its badge, so while a meeting records the lamp keeps a light on: the same tube in the same place, lit and flat, quieter than any take and deaf to the voices in the room. it does not breathe or move for as long as the meeting runs, and it is drawn once, not sixty times a second. while the meeting gets ready — until its model is in and the tap has heard the start sound — it is the ember a take wears before its mic is heard, and while a problem stands it is the same steady light in the attention colour. a pill takes the stage as always and the light is back when the pill leaves; a take allowed before the tap is heard has the lamp like any take. stop puts it out with the cool-out, while the file is still being written. it follows the meeting's phase, which the menu and the badge read too (§11). how bright it burns is one number, `HUDWaveMotion.meetingGlow`.
- **who sees it.** while a meeting is involved the panel is out of every screen capture: a share, a recording, a screenshot. that covers the meeting's light and every pill about a meeting — its three questions, `recording a meeting`, a gap, a problem the meeting records through, `writing it out…`, `saved`, and the failures. you see them; whoever is watching your share does not. dictation's lamp and pills stay in every capture, as they always were, so a screenshot of one still works in a bug report. a panel that went out of capture for a meeting stays out until it leaves the screen, so nothing on it flickers into a share mid-sentence. the menu bar badge cannot be hidden this way and shows in any share that includes the menu bar.

the rule: **a failed dictation must never look like a successful one.** anything that goes wrong cuts the afterglow short and says why. a mic that opens but sends no sound — nothing within a second (three for a bluetooth headset, which has to switch profile before it can be heard), or by key-up only zeros or under a tenth of a second — is a mic failure, not `heard nothing`: the pill names it as it names itself (`no sound from the microphone` when it won't), the next press opens a fresh one, and nothing is offered to retry, so pressing again records again. the input device changing mid-take ends the take but keeps it: what was heard up to the change is pasted, and then the pill says why the take ended without you, like the cap. sleep and the lock (the lid, ⌃⌘Q, a hot corner) end the take and keep it too (ADR 0044). it is written out and put on the clipboard, never pasted, because the field it was going to is behind the lock screen and a paste on the way back in could land in whatever you are typing by then. history keeps it if history is on. a take still being written out when the lock comes down is copied the same way, and one whose mic was still stopping when the mac slept is written out on wake. the pill waits until you unlock, or wake with no lock to get past, and so does any other pill said while the mac is away. while the mic is live the app holds the display awake (`dictating` in `pmset -g assertions`), so a long dictation, held or locked, doesn't sleep the screen under itself. it lets go the moment the mic is asked to stop.

no dock icon. menu-bar item: the brand badge → menu: fix a word…, try that again, undo learned: <word>, record a meeting and the rows a meeting brings (§11), settings, finish setup / run onboarding again, update to <x>, about, copy diagnostics, quit. `update to <x>` appears only when the daily check heard of a newer version, and never during a meeting. it gets no dot on the badge, because an old version still works. `try that again` appears only in the two minutes after the speech model threw on a dictation — the samples are still in memory, and re-running them beats saying the whole paragraph over. `undo learned: <word>` appears only in the two minutes after the app learned a word from your fixes (§3). `copy diagnostics` puts the last fifty press records on the clipboard, then the last twenty meeting records (§11) — how each press or meeting ended, the mic, the timings, never a word of what was said — and says nothing: the clipboard is the answer. like copy timings, it waits for a paste and its clipboard restore to finish, so it never lands in the middle of one. a gold dot on the badge means the mic is live for a take; a meeting wears a gold rim instead (§11). a red dot on the badge means setup is wrong — a missing permission, or a speech model that never finished downloading. both leave the app unable to transcribe a word, so both wear the dot until they are fixed.

## 5. onboarding (once, one app click)

three fixed cards — what it is, the speech models, the permissions. the first has one consent action, "set up andrew dictate (~460 mb)", which starts the downloads; the last asks macOS. the dots jump, back goes back, nothing advances on its own. nothing downloads and hotkeys remain detection-only before that click, and no later launch starts a download for a job that was not ticked.

the click starts the parakeet v2 download and warmup and prompts for accessibility; the microphone is asked for from its row. one live checklist shows microphone, accessibility, and speech-model status; download progress stays inline, denied permissions link to system settings, and model failures can retry.

pre-roll and dictionary configuration are omitted. defaults apply: fn for dictation, pre-roll off. settings owns every option. the key is not configured here, it is *proved* here: screen two shows the bound key and asks for one press, and the key picker only appears once the shipped default has visibly failed to fire.

**onboarding forks by job (ADR 0040, 2026-08-29).** the first card carries two check rows: `dictation · ~460 mb` ticked, `meeting recording · ~2.9 gb` offered unticked. the button prices what is ticked; ticking meetings adds its rows and its download, unticking dictation removes them. meeting rows in the checklist: `meeting model`, which brings whisper's tokenizer and the speaker-split models down after it (§11), and `system audio` — proved, not asked, by playing the start sound into the tap, which hears our own process like any other — that is what fires the real prompt (ADR 0021, one screen earlier). the click also makes the transcripts folder, which is what fires the ~/Documents prompt on the permissions screen instead of mid-save at the end of the first meeting; a folder it cannot make shows one more row, `transcripts folder`, and nothing appears when it can. someone who unticked meetings and later presses `record a meeting`, or the meeting shortcut, gets this window back filtered to the meeting rows, headed `set up meeting recording`. **a meetings-only setup is never nagged for accessibility**: the gate reads which jobs were chosen before it reads the permissions, because there is no hotkey to be dead and system audio is proved at every capture anyway.

**the two sizes have three homes.** `OnboardingJobs.dictationDownload` / `.meetingsDownload` are the source; the README's install section quotes them, and so does the tap cask's `caveats` in jassuwu/homebrew-tap — a second repo no release automation rewrites, which is how it spent eight releases pricing the download at 450 MB. re-price a job and all three change, or `brew install` prices it wrong before the app ever opens.

when every row of every chosen job is ready the card says "ready", shows the key and the badge, and the button becomes "start dictating"; while a model is still coming down it stays "done"; when something is missing it says "close", because nothing is done. below the checklist, behind a divider so it reads as a coda and not a fourth permission, one ticked row: `start andrew dictate when you sign in` — registered by the last button and by nothing else, hidden when macOS has no registration to make. no account, no tour, no newsletter.

**permissions are re-verified, never remembered (2026-08-13).** there is no "skip" button (ADR 0029, 2026-08-22): someone who launched the app launched it in order to set it up, and macOS already provides the exit — the window is closable. **closing is the better exit**, because it records nothing and setup returns next launch; the old link marked onboarding dismissed and silenced it for good. only "done" claims setup finished. whether the app can dictate is asked of the system at launch, at reopen, on wake and unlock, and when macOS reports a trust change. a working setup is never nagged again; a broken one gets the window back at launch or reopen, and mid-session revocation only badges the menu bar — stealing focus while you type is the sin this app exists to prevent. settings shows what's missing and routes here, because this is the only place that knows how to ask.

## 6. settings (tabs — ADR 0034, 0038, 0040)

the native `Settings` scene, five glass tabs: **dictation** (setup banner only when something is missing · hotkey · pre-roll · sound feedback · the pipeline — heard → cleanup → pasted, one word each, a switch in each stage · the dictation model cards) · **dictionary** (wrong→right pairs, a `learned` mark and a one-click remove on rows the app added, import/export json) · **history** (`dictations | meetings`, keep-new-dictations toggle, delete) · **meetings** (the meeting model cards · the meeting shortcut · save folder · how long meeting audio is kept · the hook — §11) · **general** (launch at login · the numbers dashboard · remove Andrew Dictate). the thing that opened you is where you live: nothing in settings opens another window except onboarding, which is the only surface that knows how to ask for a permission.

two rules, both learned the hard way: options that appear on more than one screen are **defined once** (`DictationOption`) and rendered twice — the copy cannot drift because there is only one copy. and the user-facing word for the asr backend is **speech model** everywhere; `engine` survives only in code (`ParakeetEngine`, `EngineVersion`). "parakeet 0.3" is a value, not a category.

## 7. instrumentation (internal)

every utterance logs its stage timestamps locally (debug menu to dump):

`keyDown → micFirstBuffer → keyUp → transcriptReady → cleaned → delivered`

working targets, not commitments: key-up → transcript ≤ 250ms, key-up → inserted ≤ 450ms (base M4, warm, p50). **the published quantity is key-up → inserted** — measured to the ⌘V keystroke reaching the target app, not to the clipboard restore that follows it; key-up → transcript would flatter us by excluding the span the claim is about (ADR 0025). "copy timings" ships in **release**: it prints p50/p95/max over delivered pastes only, with the sample size, the exclusions, and the machine/chip/os/engine/build it was measured on, above the per-utterance table it came from. anyone can produce that number on their own mac from their own speech, which is the point — it replaces the standalone bench harness rather than deferring it. **no competitor benchmark is published**: the only figure that exists anywhere is a rival founder's estimate, and a claim resting on that is not evidence.

## 8. distribution

- unsigned in v1 (ADR 0009): github releases dmg + personal tap cask `jassuwu/tap/andrew-dictate`; README documents the gatekeeper step honestly. signing + notarization + sparkle gate the "tell other people" milestone.
- **about screen:** FluidAudio (Apache-2.0) notice, parakeet weights (CC-BY-4.0) attribution, MIT license.
- **the update check (ADR 0043):** once a day, and when the menu opens on a check more than a day old, the app asks `dictate.jass.gg/api/latest?version=<running>`. the user agent and accept-language are pinned, so every copy sends the same request apart from the version. it waits out a dictation, a model load, or a meeting. offline doesn't count as the day's check; a reply without a version does. any failure shows nothing, and the automatic path never falls back to github. the endpoint (`apps/site/api/latest.ts`) reads github's `/releases/latest` redirect. its answer is `no-store`, so every check reaches it; only github's tag is cached, in memory for an hour per instance, and the last good tag is served while a lookup fails (ADR 0043, amended). for a brew install, clicking the line runs `brew upgrade --cask jassuwu/tap/andrew-dictate` in the background: the absolute brew path, no tty, a ten-minute limit. the line reads `updating…`, then `restart to finish`. a failure, a timeout, or an /Applications still holding the old version reads `couldn't update — command copied`, with the command on the clipboard and brew's last stderr line in the log. a click while dictating or recording does nothing. a dmg install, or a brew install with no brew at /opt/homebrew or /usr/local, gets the releases page. settings › general › `check for updates` turns it off, and off means no request.

## 10. open questions (parked, non-blocking)

- **branding — settled.** black + gold; the badge is the logo everywhere and is never a template image. the logo is a gold mic in aviators: the mic is the head (2026-10-03, ADR 0013). art lives in `art/`: two drawings, `logo.svg` and `logo-small.svg`, its cut for 32 px and under with no rim, rasterize to the icon set, the menu bar badge and the in-app badge via `build.sh`, which then runs `og-compose.swift` for the og image and copies og/badge/favicons into `apps/site/public`. taglines: "escape the keyboard." + "free · open source · fully local".
- **website — shipped (2026-08-11):** dictate.jass.gg, astro, in `apps/site`.
- **HUD placement/personality — decided (2026-08-12):** bottom-centre, the lamp. see §4.
- ~~dictation history beyond "copy last" — deliberately absent~~ **superseded 2026-08-22 (ADR 0022).** two durable nouns: a **dictation** is your own speech and is kept until you delete it (raw + inserted text, 0600 on disk, no record of which app you were in); a **meeting recording** holds other people's words and is a separate type with its own retention rules, decided alongside meeting capture: the transcript stays until you delete it, and the audio a day by default (§11, ADR 0048). the old test — "revisit only if losing a transcript hurts" — was measured and fails: nothing is lost, because a bad dictation gets fixed in place. history exists because a tool used a hundred times a day should leave a trace, not as a safety net.
- pre-roll buffer depth (~300ms is a starting guess) — tune once real first-word-loss data exists.
- per-channel language in a meeting — open (2026-09-12). translating *them* while leaving *you* as spoken needs the two sides decoded apart, which every meeting model now does, a stretch at a time, and two sides of large-v3 keep pace live (measured 2026-10: the decoder busy a third of the time in ordinary talk and six tenths in heavy overlap, the queue never growing). what is missing is the choice, not the speed: where it is set, and what it defaults to. until then the model decides for the whole room, and §11 says so.

## 11. meetings (ADR 0021, 0023, 0040, 0047, 0048, 0049)

a local meeting recorder. your mic is *you*; everything the mac plays is *them* — the call, whatever app it is in, and a video or a notification sound if one plays while it runs (ADR 0049). what comes out is **one markdown transcript**, in english with the default model, checked against the audio before the audio goes. the audio stays on this mac for a day by default, out of the transcripts folder, and is then deleted; a transcript that comes out thin keeps its audio until you delete it (ADR 0048).

- **start / stop:** `record a meeting` in the menu, one item: there is no app to pick, because the tap hears the whole mac. the same start is `record with ▸`, the reminder's `record` and the meeting shortcut, all below; the same stop is `stop recording` in the menu, the shortcut, or `stop` on a question the pill asks. manual both ends: the app watches which apps use the mic so it can ask, and it never acts on what it sees. nothing starts or stops a recording but you. a meeting started while a call is on is named after the call (`zoom`), and otherwise `meeting`. a mac without the meeting model settings chose gets setup instead (§5). the lamp says `recording a meeting` once the tap is heard (§4); from the press until the stop the menu offers `stop recording` and `live transcript` under its first line, and hides `fix a word…`, `record a meeting` and the rows that come and go beside them. dictation is refused while the meeting records, a rebuild included, and the pill says `recording a meeting — stop it to dictate`; a take before the tap is heard is an ordinary take. a press of record mid-take gets `finish dictating first`, because the mic is one. the stop hands the meeting to its write-out at once, so the next can start while the last is still being written. a quit, from the menu, ⌘Q or brew asking to replace the app, stops the meeting and waits for every file still being written, 20 s at most; what that cuts off is recovered at the next launch.
- **record with:** under `record a meeting`, and only where it is, `record with ▸` lists the other meeting models on this mac by name, and is there only when there is one; choosing one starts a meeting named as the menu item would name it and heard by that model for that meeting alone — the file's `engine`, the spool and a recovery all say which — while the default in settings, the next `record a meeting`, the reminder's `record` and the shortcut keep using the default. a model gone since the menu was drawn says `whisper turbo is not on this mac` and starts nothing.
- **the meeting shortcut (ADR 0047):** unset until you set one, in settings › meetings › *meeting shortcut*, where you click the chip and press a combination with ⌃ or ⌘ in it (⌘W or ⌘Q alone leaves instead, as it would anywhere). a press does what the menu does: it starts a meeting, sends a mac with no meeting model to setup, or stops the one that records. a meeting still writing its file is not recording, so a press then starts the next. it is registered with the system as a hot key, which asks for no accessibility, so it never touches the dictation key's monitor. a combination that holds the dictation key's modifier, is on esc, holds ⌥ without ⌃ or ⌘ (macOS 15 and later will not register it), is ⌘ or ⌘⇧ on a key every mac app answers (a, c, f, h, m, n, o, p, q, s, t, v, w, x, z, tab, space, return, delete), or is ⌘⇧3, 4 or 5 is refused on the row with a few words why. a combination the system will not register stays set, and the row says `another app already uses that.` when another app holds it and `can't use that one.` otherwise.
- **the reminder (ADR 0047):** the app watches one thing, which processes hold the mic and which play audio, read from Core Audio's process objects, and the **call watcher** decides from it when a call begins and ends. it needs no new permission and reads no window title, tab address or calendar. a listener on every input device, not just the default, says when anyone takes a mic, so a call on a usb mic or airpods while the default is the mac's own is heard too, and a mic plugged in later is listened to from when it arrives. only then, while a meeting records, or while a call is on, is the process list read: every two seconds, or every ten while the mic is held by this app alone (pre-roll keeps it open, a dictation takes it). nobody on a call costs listeners and nothing else, and a mac without the meeting model settings chose does not even listen: parakeet being on disk for dictation's v3 is not a mac set up for meetings. picking a model in settings starts or stops the listening to match, and a model finishing its download starts it. the call apps are zoom, teams, slack, facetime and the browsers (chrome, safari, arc, firefox, brave, edge), a helper process counting for its app. this app's own process never counts, so dictation and a meeting's own capture are never a call. one call app holding the mic and playing audio for three seconds is a call, and a second app is not looked at until it ends. a call is still read after its app lets go of the mic, because a call app that closes the mic on mute is still playing everyone else, and that call goes on, for ten minutes after the app was last on the mic. past that its audio alone no longer keeps the call, because a call that ended and a video played in the same browser afterwards look the same. with nothing recording, the pill asks `call in zoom — record it?` with a `record` button for 15 seconds, once per call. `record` starts a meeting exactly as the menu does, named after the call, so the file, its front matter and the hook say `zoom`. a click beside the button is a no for that call. after that the menu leads with `call in zoom · not recording` above `record a meeting` until the call ends, and the menu bar icon shows it. a browser tab using the mic for something that is not a call will be asked about; the cost is a pill that leaves after 15 seconds. the 15 and 30 second values and the ten minutes are guesses until real calls tune them.
- **the call ending:** a call ends when its app has done neither for 30 seconds, or has been off the mic for 30 seconds past its ten minutes on audio alone. while a recording runs, that asks once, `call ended — stop recording?`, with `stop`, for 30 seconds. a click beside the button, or no answer, leaves the recording running. nothing stops by itself, and that call is not asked about again. a meeting with no call in it never gets this question; it still gets the nudge.
- **the nudge:** an hour in which the tap hears nothing (the far side, not your mic, and none of our own tones) asks `still recording?` twice: through a notification with `keep going` / `stop`, and on the pill with `stop` for 30 seconds, so it reaches someone who has notifications off. a click on the pill beside the button is `keep going`. the first answer, from either, is the one acted on, and the other is withdrawn; `keep going` starts the hour again. it asks; it never acts.
- **a start that fails** says which part failed and points at that part's fix. the mic permission is asked of the mac before anything is built or loaded, every meeting, so a grant taken back is caught for a setup that was meetings only too: not allowed → `the mic isn't allowed — opening setup`, and setup opens at its permissions. a spool that cannot be made, a full disk or a folder that will not take a file → `can't write the meeting's audio to disk — …`, and no window: nothing in setup fixes a disk. no mic at all, one that would not start or did not within eight seconds, or one that started and delivered nothing, and the built-in mic tried after it delivered nothing either (proving it can hear, below) → `can't start your mic — yeti`, or `no mic to record you with`, and no window: nothing in setup starts a mic. the tap → `can't hear the mac — opening setup` (below), and so does a failure that does not say which part it was. none of these keeps anything, and each leaves its own reason in the record. a model that will not load, at the start or seconds into the meeting, is `meeting model failed — …`: the recording stops, and its spool stays for the next launch to write out.
- **proving it can hear:** capture starts by playing the start sound, and the tap must hear it within a second and a half (ADR 0021). never heard → `can't hear the mac — opening setup`, which opens setup at its permissions, and the menu keeps a `fix system audio…` row until a meeting is heard; the menu never says `recording` over a tap that has not delivered. with no output to play the start sound on, the tap was asked nothing: the meeting records, and the quiet probe asks it later. the tap and the mic come up together, in one private aggregate device on the mic's clock, built off the main thread with eight seconds to come up and five to go: a tap that never opens is `can't hear`, and one that never closes is left behind while the file is written anyway. a mic that comes up and hands over no audio at all — a stuck headset, an interface with no clock — is given the second and a half and three more, on the wall clock, then the built-in mic is tried in its place, with the start sound again; nothing from that either and the start fails as the mic's, `can't start your mic — yeti`, nothing kept. a tap that worked and then went quiet is below: silence is not damage, and a dead tap.
- **the mic:** whatever the mac says the default input is when you press record, or the built-in mic if that is none a meeting can use. it follows the default input from then on: airpods connecting, a usb mic plugged in, a pick in control centre, or the mic going away (airpods dying) moves the meeting to the new default, or to the built-in mic when there is none. the move waits for the hardware to settle, a second and a half after the last change and three at most after the first, and is one move however many changes came. a new tap and mic come up beside the old ones, which keep recording until the new pair delivers; there is no start sound, nothing on the lamp, and the meeting's clock carries on: no hole while the old mic is still recording, and a mic that goes from under its device leaves the far side coming through it, your side silent, until the new pair delivers. more than a second in which nothing came from either side is a gap, kept with its timestamps like a dead tap's, over by the time it is known, and the file says `complete: false`; under a second is hardware jitter and left alone. a new mic that never delivers is given five seconds and dropped, and the meeting stays where it was, or, if its mic has gone, tries the built-in one. a mic with several inputs is heard on the ones carrying sound; an aggregate input (audio midi setup) is heard through the mics inside it. every move, a move that failed and a fall back to the built-in mic go in the meeting's record. a muted mic is not a fault (problems, below).
- **silence is not damage:** a quiet far side is never rebuilt, gapped or chirped at for being quiet, however long it lasts — presenting to a muted room sounds exactly like a dead tap. only when it has been silent for two minutes while something other than this app is playing is the tap asked again, the way it was at the start: the **quiet probe**, a third of a second of 1 khz at -40 dbfs, which it hears if it hears anything, given two seconds. heard, nothing happens and nothing is said, and the next question waits another two minutes; not heard, the tap is dead. whether anything but this app is playing is asked of the HAL once a second, off the main thread, and no answer counts as nothing playing. a tone that cannot be played at all is no evidence either way. our tones reach the far side like any sound, so the model is handed silence in their place: they are never transcribed, and never count as the room speaking. a tap that stops calling back — sleep, a locked screen, a dead driver — is dead too, found by the wall clock rather than by audio time, which stops with it: five seconds with nothing across a wake or an unlock, two minutes otherwise. a tap that comes back on its own before that — the lid shut, the mac asleep, and the buffers flowing again before anything was found — leaves the time it was out as a gap by the buffers' own timestamps: more than a second from one to the next is skipped on the meeting's clock and kept as a gap, nothing said on the lamp, since it is over by the time it is known.
- **a dead tap:** a gap from where the far side was last heard — the silence since then was no more proven than the tap is now — kept with its timestamps, and the file says `complete: false`; the lamp says `lost the other side — rebuilding`. the tap is rebuilt once the hardware has had a second and a half to settle, tried three times in a row two then five seconds apart, and trusted again only once it hears its own start sound, within a second and a half (`hearing them again`). three tries and still nothing is a **problem**, not the end: the lamp says `can't hear the call — still recording your side`, your side goes on being recorded, the gap stays open, and the tap is tried every thirty seconds until one works — `hearing the call again` — or the meeting is stopped, and its gap runs to the end of the file. every try builds the new tap and mic beside whatever is delivering now, which goes only once the new pair has delivered and the start sound has been played through it, so your side is never lost to a try. your side is recorded by the mic alone: the first rebuild that fails brings the mic up on its own beside the old pair, in an aggregate with no tap, through the same chunks with the far side as silence and on the meeting's one clock, and it follows the default input as the tap and mic together do; if the mic alone will not come up either, the old pair goes on recording your side. if nothing is being delivered — not even the mic alone would come up, or nothing has arrived since the tries began — the lamp says `can't hear the call or your mic — still trying` instead, and the try after one that delivered nothing at all is on the built-in mic. no problem during a call opens a window or takes focus. the mac is kept from idle sleep while a meeting records; the display may sleep. every number here is a guess until real meetings tune it.
- **problems that stay:** several can stand at once, one of each kind, the worst first: the call unheard, your mic unheard, the audio not saved, the disk nearly full. each is said on the lamp when it begins and again when it clears, each clears on its own, and none opens a window; the worst is the menu's first line and the badge's mark until it does (below). **your mic:** the mic handing over nothing but silence — under 0.0001 rms, far below the hiss of any real room — for ten seconds while the far side talks is `can't hear your mic — macbook pro microphone`, named as the mac names it, until the mic is heard again (`hearing your mic again`). both sides silent is a quiet room and says nothing. **a muted mic:** the mic's own mute switch on, or its input volume at nothing — read from the mic every two seconds, off the main thread — is silent on purpose and not a fault: nothing is held against it while it lasts, a mic problem standing is over, and the lamp says `your mic is muted`, once the meeting is recording if it was muted before. **the audio:** a chunk the spool will not take is `can't save the audio — still transcribing`: the live transcript goes on, every chunk refused is counted, and the first it takes again ends it (`saving the audio again`). **the disk:** under a gigabyte free on the disk the spool is on, when the meeting starts or at a look once a minute after, is `disk nearly full`; the meeting records all the same, and a later look that finds room ends it (`the disk has room again`).
- **what the menu and the badge show:** one **phase** of the meeting, the one the lamp follows too (§4), and the menu's first line says it in words. **getting ready**, from the press until the meeting's model has loaded and the tap has heard the start sound: `getting ready…`, and a gold rim part of the way round the badge. once the tap is heard the audio is kept, so a stop while the model still loads keeps the words: the file waits for the model, a minute at most. the pill says `recording a meeting` once the tap is heard, as it always has; a model still loading three seconds after the press, with the tap not heard yet either, says `getting ready…` once before it, and never after it, so a parakeet start says nothing extra. **recording:** `recording · 12:34`, and the full gold rim — the mic is live, and it is a meeting, not a take. a tap being rebuilt with no problem standing is still recording. **a problem**, the worst of those standing: the lamp's words for it, `can't hear your mic — macbook pro microphone`, until it clears, and a red triangle with a black `!` in the badge's corner. **writing it out**, from the stop until the file is on disk: `writing it out…`, and the partial rim again — the badge is still busy with a meeting — while the lamp has cooled out. a recording a crash left, written out at launch, is the same phase and says `writing out an unsaved zoom recording…`; a meeting stopped before the tap was heard has nothing to write and goes straight back to the bare badge. then `show last meeting in finder` leads for ten minutes. with no meeting, a call nobody records is `call in zoom · not recording` and gold corner brackets. the meeting being recorded is the one shown, whatever one stopped earlier is doing. voiceover reads the badge as the phase in words. the three seconds are a guess.
- **what the lamp says**, on pills a screen share never sees (§4), each a moment you would otherwise miss or a failure that must not look like success. at the start: `getting ready…` (once, for a slow model), `recording a meeting`, or the failure by name (above). while it records: `lost the other side — rebuilding` and `hearing them again` around a gap, each problem's line and its clearing (`hearing the call again`, `hearing your mic again`, `saving the audio again`, `the disk has room again`), and `your mic is muted`. at the end: `writing it out…`, `the transcript looked thin — reading the audio again…` when the coverage check asks for it, then `saved · 1h 42m`, `saved · 2 gaps` or `saved · incomplete, audio kept`; or `nothing was heard, nothing kept` (stopped before the tap was heard), `couldn't save the transcript — <why>. kept for next launch`, and `hook failed (exit 3)`. at launch: `found an unsaved zoom recording — writing it out…`, then `recovered zoom — saved · 1h 42m`. for a rerun: `transcribing again with whisper large…`, then `transcribed again · whisper large`, or `couldn't transcribe again — <why>. the transcript is as it was`. the menu's own refusals, `finish dictating first` and `whisper turbo is not on this mac`, are kept from a share the same way.
- **models:** each job picks its own model from the same cards; the card states the consequence (whisper for dictation is slow; parakeet for meetings has no hindi). for meetings: whisper large-v3 via WhisperKit, the default and the one that translates; whisper turbo, the smaller card, which cannot translate; **or parakeet v3**, the model dictation already offers, via FluidAudio: the fastest card, with nothing to download for anyone who dictates with v3, and it knows english and the european languages only — hindi, or anything else, comes out as nonsense. the meeting loads a model of its own and lets it go when it ends; dictation's parakeet is never borrowed. the meeting model downloads at onboarding when the job is ticked, or the first time you press record, and what a meeting reads besides it comes down with it — whisper's tokenizer, and the speaker-split models — so no meeting waits on the network, at its start or at its end. silero is in the app (1 mb, MIT) and is never fetched; if it will not load, a meeting tells speech by loudness. a mac set up before these came down with the model is the one exception: a meeting there fetches whichever is missing, the way setup would have. the tokenizer comes before the model loads, waited on for 30 s at most, and the meeting says `meeting model failed` only if it still cannot be read; the speaker-split models come down beside the meeting, which leaves its `them` unsplit, and the next meeting has them.
- **reading it, a stretch at a time:** every model hears the two channels apart: silero, FluidAudio's voice model, which ships inside the app, finds where speech begins and ends on each, and each **stretch** of speech is decoded once, when it ends — at most 25 s for whisper and 15 s for parakeet, and talk that runs longer is cut at a quiet moment just before that. a stretch is a line in the live view and a turn in the file, labelled by the side it came from, so a label is never a guess. a stretch the model throws on, or does not answer in time (ten seconds, and a second more for each second of it), gets one more try; a second failure is counted in the record, never only logged. a `you` stretch that is only the far side coming back through the mic on the mac's own speakers — its loudness rising and falling as theirs did a moment before, while they were talking through most of it — is let go before it is decoded, so they are said once, as `them`; when it isn't clear the stretch is kept, because a doubled line costs less than a lost one, and none under a second is judged. what whisper writes over room noise (`thank you`, `thanks for watching`) is dropped from a stretch that quiet, and its `[BLANK_AUDIO]` marks come out. the language is the model's choice and not the speaker's: large-v3 translates every stretch into english, your own hindi included; turbo cannot translate, so it writes every language as spoken. speakers are labelled per channel; language is not. **the stretches read live are the transcript**: nothing is read twice unless the coverage check finds it thin. stop waits for the model and the stretches still queued, a minute at most, and what is never read is counted for the check. after stop the file is on disk in seconds, however long the meeting ran.
- **them 1, them 2:** the **speaker split**, FluidAudio's diarizer, hears the far side a minute at a time while the meeting records, in the background, where it never competes with the reading, so stop has only the last minute left for it and gives it ten seconds at most before the file is written without it. a piece it throws on gets a second try at the stop, and a piece that finds more than five waiting is let go. the speakers are numbered 1, 2, 3 in the order they first speak in the file, none skipped; one voice leaves plain `them`, and so does a stretch the diarizer could not hear. a spool a crash left, and kept audio read again, are heard after, a minute at a time.
- **live view:** a floating glass panel, open for your first meeting and remembered after that; toggled from the menu (`live transcript`, `hide live transcript`); interleaved `you` / `them` lines with timestamps, each a finished stretch as it is decoded, the far side plain `them` until the file numbers it. nothing on it is dimmed or rewritten. it is out of every screen capture, always: it holds other people's words, and a share of your screen does not show it.
- **who sees what:** you see the lamp's light, the meeting's pills and the live view; a screen share, a recording or a screenshot sees none of them (§4). a share that includes the menu bar still shows the badge's gold rim, and macOS's own microphone indicator beside it. measured on macOS 27 against `screencapture`, ScreenCaptureKit's screenshots and live streams, and single-window capture; a share from zoom itself is still to be checked.
- **the coverage check (ADR 0048):** at stop, before any audio is deleted, what was transcribed is held against what was said. the stretch transcriber knows, per side, the seconds of speech it cut (the far side coming back through the mic, let go, is not counted) and how much of that the model read; the spool says on its own, a block at a time, how long the far side was louder than the silence floor. a side with 10 s of speech or more and over a fifth of it unread is thin (`some of what was said could not be read`); a side with 30 s read or more and under half a word a second of it is thin (`far fewer words than the talk that was heard`); a minute of the far side in the spool with no speech cut from it is thin (`the other side was heard and nothing of it was read`). the numbers are provisional, in one place, and every result goes in the record. thin → the lamp says `the transcript looked thin — reading the audio again…`, a fresh transcriber for the same model reads the whole spool, and that reading is checked on its own numbers: passing, it is the transcript; still thin, or thrown, the reading with more words is written with `complete: false` and the reason, and that meeting's audio is kept until you delete it. either way the file is written once, and nothing replaces it but your asking for it to be transcribed again (below). a meeting nobody spoke in passes, and its body says `> nobody spoke`. a recovered spool is checked and not read again — it already was.
- **the file:** `<folder you picked>/meetings/2026-08/2026-08-29-1402-meeting.md`, default parent `~/andrew-dictate/`, no spaces — deliberately not `~/Documents` or `~/Desktop`, which icloud syncs; a folder you pick that does sync says so on the row. written once, atomically. front matter: app (`meeting`, or the call app's name when a call was on at the start: `zoom`), started, ended, duration_s, engine, speakers (in order of first appearance, the labels the body uses), words, complete, reason (only when incomplete: `audio was lost in 2 gaps` unless something else says why), gaps, recovered. the body is one paragraph per speaker turn, `[hh:mm:ss] you: …`: a speaker's consecutive lines are one paragraph stamped with the first, and a monologue gets a new paragraph a minute after the last one began, so there is always a time to find your place by; talk that picks up more than 3 s after the last line ended is a new paragraph too. `them 1` and `them 2` are different speakers and never merge. a transcript with gaps also says in its body where they are, because nobody reads front matter. files from before this layout (no `ended`, `speakers` or `words`, a line per fragment) are still read as they are. `meetings/README.md` sits beside the month folders and tells an agent what the files are and what each key means: written once, when a transcript is saved and none is there, never rewritten or overwritten, never a row in history. kept until you delete it — in finder or in history, where the row opens it: `open`, or a double-click, hands the markdown to whatever editor you already keep. there is no reader of ours — `live transcript` exists only while the meeting is running. the file is 0600 and the two folders the app makes for it 0700; the folder you picked is left as it is. **the saved moment:** the lamp says `saved · 1h 42m`, a notification names the file and offers `show in finder` — the pill cannot be clicked and is gone before you are back at the mac — and for ten minutes the menu leads with `show last meeting in finder`. a write that failed says so and offers no button, because there is no file yet.
- **kept audio (ADR 0048):** once the file is written, the spool is compressed — AAC in an `.m4a`, 16 kHz, `you` and `them` still two channels, written a block at a time — into `meeting-audio/` in application support, 0600 inside 0700, never the transcripts folder, and the spool goes. beside it a small json says which transcript it belongs to, when the meeting started, the model, and until when it is kept, or that it is kept until you delete it. a spool that will not compress is kept as it was, `.caf`. settings › meetings › *keep meeting audio*: `delete at once`, `one day` (the default) or `seven days`, read when a meeting starts. `delete at once` deletes it the moment the file is written, as before. a thin transcript's audio is kept until you delete it, whatever the setting — until its transcript is moved, renamed or deleted outside the app, when no row is left to delete it from: then it is given a week, and goes like any other. past its date it is deleted without asking: at launch — one look at one folder, nothing built for meetings — after every meeting, and once a day while the app runs. a meeting whose audio exists says so on its history row, `audio until fri 14:02` or `audio kept`, which offers `delete audio now`; deleting the transcript there deletes its audio. the app quitting while it keeps the audio loses nothing: the next launch finishes the job and never writes the meeting out twice. removing the app's data takes it with the spool. an hour of it is about 25 mb, and takes about four seconds to compress.
- **transcribe again (ADR 0048):** while a meeting's audio is kept its history row offers `transcribe again with ▸` and every meeting model that is installed, the one that wrote the transcript included, since the same model again is a fair retry. it is off while a meeting is being recorded, while another is being transcribed again, or with no meeting model installed, and the row says which (`recording`, `one at a time`, `no model installed`); the row shows `transcribing again…` while it runs, and the action is not there once the audio is gone. the kept audio is read whole, as a recovery's spool is, by a fresh transcriber for the model you picked, checked by the coverage check and not read again, its far side split into speakers, and the new reading replaces the transcript file in place — atomically, at the same path. it is the one time a file changes after it is written, and it changes because you asked: `engine`, `speakers`, `words`, `complete`, `reason` and the body are the new reading's; `app`, `started`, `ended`, `duration_s`, `gaps` and `recovered` stay as the file says. if the model throws or will not load, the audio is gone or empty, the file was deleted meanwhile, or the new reading is thin where the file was whole or finds nobody speaking where the file has words, the file is left exactly as it was, the lamp says `couldn't transcribe again — <why>. the transcript is as it was`, and the audio stays. a thin file whose new reading passes is an ordinary meeting's now: its audio is kept as long as the setting says, counted from then; every other audio date stays where it was, and a rerun never deletes audio. the lamp says `transcribing again with whisper large…`, then `transcribed again · whisper large`; the hook runs again with `again: true`; and the meeting record gets one record per rerun, marked `again=1`, with the model, the coverage result, turns and words per side and the seconds it took — its outcome `unchanged` when the file was left as it was.
- **hook:** settings → meetings → *after a meeting is saved, run:* one executable chosen by file picker. invoked detached once the file is closed: transcript path as `$1`, json on stdin (`event`, `transcript`, `folder`, `app`, `started_at`, `duration_s`, `complete`, `gaps`, `recovered`, `again`), the same as `ANDREW_*` env. `again` is `true` (`ANDREW_AGAIN=1`) when the file was replaced because its meeting was transcribed again from its kept audio, and the hook runs a second time for it so an agent's copy does not go stale; on a first save it is `false` (`ANDREW_AGAIN=0`). no timeout; output to `hooks.log`. a failure is announced once on the hud and stays on the row as `last run: … · exit 3 · open log`; a success stays as `last run: … · ok`, so you can tell a hook that ran from one that never fired. the only event is `meeting-saved`.
- **recovery:** a spool orphaned by a crash is transcribed at the next launch, five seconds in, and saved flagged `recovered`, with the gaps, the length and the stamps the meeting had: its manifest notes each gap as it begins and ends, with how much audio the spool held then, and how long the meeting had run, and the stop notes them once more, so a crash, a quit cut short or a write that failed is not handed back looking whole. it is checked like any meeting, and not read again (the coverage check, above). it reads one spool at a time, waits while a meeting records, and never takes the spool of a meeting being recorded or written out. recovery never deletes a meeting. audio it cannot read, and a folder with audio whose manifest will not decode, are set aside in the spool's `unreadable` folder, not deleted — only audio that reads and has no samples in it is let go, because nothing was captured. a spool whose model is gone is read with another that is installed, whisper large then turbo then parakeet, and the file's `engine` names the one that wrote it; with none installed the spool waits for a launch that has one, and no attempt is counted. two failed launches set a spool aside too. settings › history says how many are set aside (`2 recordings couldn't be transcribed`) and offers `show in finder` and `try again`: each recording comes back to the spool with its tries cleared and is read once more, one that fails again goes straight back aside, and a folder that has no manifest is given one — `meeting`, the audio's own date, the default model.
- **the record:** every meeting leaves exactly one **meeting record** of how it ended, whatever the ending, the way a press does (§4), and every transcribe again leaves one more: saved; saved thin (audio kept); nothing kept (the tap never heard, you stopped first, the mic not allowed, no mic that would start, no spool); the model failed (audio kept); the transcript could not be written (audio kept); for a recovery, one that failed and was counted, one set aside, audio it could not read and set aside, a spool waiting for a model, a spool with nothing in it; and for a rerun, `unchanged`. it holds the app, the model, when it started and how long it ran from its first audio, the gaps and the seconds they took, turns and words per side, the seconds from stop to the file, whether it was a recovery or a rerun, and what the meeting went through by its own clock: a gap began and ended, a rebuild failed, a tone of ours heard, unheard or unplayable, each problem begun and cleared, the mic moved, a move that failed, a fall back to the built-in mic, the mic muted and unmuted, the mic alone or not even that, a time nothing came from either side, and a mic that delivered nothing at all. besides those, the decoding (stretches read per side, those that failed twice, and how far behind live it ran, at worst and last), the coverage check (its result and reason, speech and unread seconds per side, the stretches let go as the far side on the mic, how long the far side was loud), whether the audio was kept and until when, the speaker split's seconds after the stop and the pieces it let go, and how many chunks the spool refused. never a word of what was said, and no path to the file. one notice-level line each in the unified log, and the last two hundred in `meeting-records.jsonl` beside `presses.jsonl`: 0600, taken with your dictations when you remove the app's data, and a record written before a field existed still reads. `copy diagnostics` hands over the last twenty.

# andrew dictate — v1 spec

> hold a key, talk, get text.
> free forever. fully local. small enough to read.

decisions in this spec are backed by ADRs kept in `docs/adr/` and research in `~/repos/personal/andrew-dictate-research/` — **both are local-only and deliberately untracked** (see `.gitignore`), a private archive of how each decision was reached, including the ones later reversed. if you cloned this repo, you have the spec, not the archive; nothing here depends on reading it. terms are defined in `GLOSSARY.md` and used exactly.

## 1. product

- **name:** Andrew Dictate. binary/app: `Andrew Dictate.app`, cask `andrew-dictate`, repo `jassuwu/andrew-dictate` (MIT, public day one — ADR 0010).
- **platform:** macOS 26+, Apple Silicon only. *(was 14+ until 0.8.0; the glass windows and the meeting tap both need 26, and a floor that moves once is better than two features that each say "not on this mac".)*
- **thesis:** frontier-fast dictation, with the smallest possible surface: no account, no cloud, no settings maze, no subscription. trust is architectural. the app goes online in three places and no others: the model download, the about window's `check for updates`, and the daily update check, which sends dictate.jass.gg the running version and nothing else (ADR 0043).
- **non-goals (v1):** windows/linux, iOS, always-on listening (deferred — ADR 0003), ~~meeting transcription~~ (**crossed 2026-08-22 — ADR 0023**: manual start and stop, the app never observes which processes hold the mic), ~~history browser~~ (**superseded — ADR 0022**), App Store.
- **voice command mode: removed (2026-08-06).** shipped in early v1 (router tiers, agent delegation, ask/screen-ask), cut entirely to focus the product on dictation. this spec describes the app as it is; command-mode sections and terms are gone from here and the glossary.

## 2. the pipeline

one pipeline, one sink (ADR 0003):

```
hold fn ──▶ mic capture ──▶ key-up ──▶ engine (parakeet v2, prewarmed)
                                              │ transcript
                                              ▼ deterministic cleaner (always)
                                              ▼ inserter (paste into frontmost app)
```

- **engine:** parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2 int8 via FluidAudio, batch on key-up, prewarmed at launch with one dummy inference (ADR 0002, 0007). v3 optional in settings. engine sits behind an `Engine` protocol (streaming engines are additive later). **nothing waits on it forever:** a take gets four seconds, or a quarter of its length once that is longer. a take the engine misses ends `couldn't transcribe — tap to try again` with the samples kept for a tap, and whatever the engine says later goes nowhere. the app then asks the engine a second of silence off the main thread and reloads the model in the background if that goes unanswered too. a second missed take in a row says `speech model isn't responding — restarting it` and reloads it. a reload that fails, or is still going after a minute, is retried by the next press, out loud. **awake by key-up:** a press wakes the engine with half a second of silence while you talk, unless it ran in the last ten seconds — the neural engine idles down between dictations, and a take handed to it cold waits on it. a take never runs beside a wake still in flight; it waits for it. at key-up the engine is asked first, and the chime, the lamp, the target and the clipboard snapshot all happen while it works.
- **capture:** AVAudioEngine, hardware-native format, graph prepared at launch. **capture is disposable:** each one owns one engine and one serial queue, and the main thread never touches the engine — it awaits, and gives up after ~1.5 s (`microphone isn't responding`). a fresh engine opens the system default input by name. a default input or output change, a device list change, a wake or a display change makes it stale; the next press gets a fresh one, and after ~500 ms of quiet the stale one is thrown away and a fresh one built ready — engine made, default mic bound, graph prepared, no i/o started — so the first press after a monitor or the lid starts warm (with pre-roll on, it starts listening). nothing is ever rebuilt in place. the mic's first audio, which lights the lamp and plays the chime, is reported by a sink node called every i/o cycle; the take itself is captured by the tap. **pre-roll is a user toggle (ADR 0012), chosen at onboarding:** ON = a ~300ms rolling ring buffer runs while the app is active (mic stays open, indicator stays lit, buffer lives only in memory and is discarded continuously) so the first word is never clipped; OFF = mic starts at key-down, maximum privacy posture. changeable in settings.
- **cleaner (deterministic, always on — ADR 0004, 0019, 0020):** eight staged transforms — whitespace normalization, dictionary substitutions, spoken punctuation, email/url/number parsing, capitalization, punctuation finishing. **the dictionary runs before the parsers** so it reads the engine's own words: the word you point at in "fix a word…" is the word an entry matches, whether cleanup is on or off. **it renders what you said into how it is written, and never decides you meant something else** (ADR 0020): the three stages that edited rather than transcribed — self-corrections, repetition collapse, filler removal — are gone, because each of them could silently change correct speech into something that still read perfectly. stumbles reach the page. microseconds, no model, pure functions. not user-disableable: it is where the dictionary and spoken punctuation live, and without it you get raw asr. **a dictation aimed at Andrew Dictate's own window runs the dictionary only** — a correction is a word, not a sentence, so "cache" is not saved as "Cache."
- **hotkeys (ADR 0008):** fn = dictation, rebindable, chord-cancel semantics. NSEvent flagsChanged monitoring; no Input Monitoring permission. **locked recording:** double-tap the dictation key to lock its capture hands-free; a single tap ends it and inserts as normal. the whole gesture is written under `key` in settings › dictation — hold, double-tap to lock, one tap to end, esc to cancel — so the lock is read rather than stumbled into.

## 3. dictation

- key-up → transcript → cleaner → **inserter**.
- **insertion strategy (v1): transactional paste only.** snapshot pasteboard (all types) — read off the main thread while the engine works, and read again at the paste if anything was copied in between — write plain text, synthetic cmd-V resolved for the active layout, verify change, restore only if `changeCount` still ours. the transcript is marked `org.nspasteboard.TransientType` so clipboard managers do not keep a copy of it. AX selected-text insertion is v1.x.
- **target safety:** frontmost bundle id + focused-element captured at key-up (aiming at the field you want while still talking is the normal thing to do); re-verified before paste. focus changed → don't paste; transcript stays on the clipboard + HUD shows "copied — focus changed · ⌘V to paste." every copied-instead pill carries the verb: the panel cannot be clicked, so the recovery has to be something the hands already know.
- **secure fields:** detected via AX subrole → never auto-insert; HUD offers explicit copy. a dictation refused for a secure field is never written to `dictations.jsonl`, and its clipboard copy is marked `org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType` so clipboard managers do not record it.
- **what it keeps (ADR 0022, 0026):** every delivered dictation is written to `dictations.jsonl` in application support — raw + inserted text, time, engine, key-up→inserted — **on by default**, stated in onboarding, with the toggle and `delete all` in settings. cancelled dictations are not kept; there was no text. chmod 0600. this is a deliberate contrast with pre-roll's off-by-default: pre-roll opens a microphone, this keeps text already produced and already pasted.
- **ceiling:** capture stops itself at five minutes and keeps what it heard; the pill says `five minutes — that's the cap`. **esc** cancels anything in flight — nothing pasted, nothing kept.
- **learning from your fixes (ADR 0046):** after a delivered dictation the app watches the words it pasted, in that field, for a minute, and nothing else. each read is our words plus 40 characters either side; our words are found again by their first and last words, and when neither is there the dictation is given up on. the watch ends after 60 s, when focus leaves the field, when our words are gone, or at the next press. password fields and the app's own windows are never watched. a change counts only if it replaces one to three words we pasted with one to three that sound like them. added or deleted words, rewrites, case, punctuation, inflections like `large` → `larger` and swaps between common words like `there` → `their` are your writing, not a mishearing. fixes are counted in memory, one per dictation, and a restart forgets them. the second identical fix becomes a dictionary entry keyed on the engine's own words, and only if that entry gives back the text you fixed. it is marked `learned`. the pill says `learned: <word>` once — learned during a meeting or a take, it is said when the pill is free, even after the two minutes. the menu offers `undo learned: <word>` for two minutes, and the dictionary tab shows the mark beside a one-click remove. removing a learned entry from either place means it is never learned again; the pair is kept in `never-learn.json` beside the dictionary. a learned entry is an ordinary substitution and yours like any other. the cleaner's rules are untouched (ADR 0020).
- **escape hatch:** ~~"copy last" in the menu~~ **removed 2026-08-22 (ADR 0030).** it was justified as the way out when a paste fails — but a failed paste *already* leaves the text on the clipboard ("copied — secure field", "copied — focus changed"), so the hatch was covering a hole that was already covered. wanting it again is what the archive is for — so the archive row carries `copy`, which puts the inserted text (not the raw) on the clipboard and reads `copied` for 1.2 s. the raw transcript is still held, and still feeds "fix a word…" — which is the one action the menu keeps, because it is the only one that is time-sensitive.

## 4. hud

one nonactivating `NSPanel` (borderless, floating, all-spaces) that is never key and lets every click through, except over a pill with a button: an invisible stage, sized once per screen for the widest line of text, never morphing. everything visible is Liquid Glass inside it, in one container, so a change of state is a change of shape:

- **the tube (2026-09-22).** the lamp is a 6-point glass tube, 112 wide, drawn by hand in the canvas: a translucent tan body that takes the backdrop through it, a pale rim along the top, a shade along the bottom, a faint outline so it holds on white, a core that comes up with your voice, a blurred halo spilling by loudness — dim ember while prewarming for a key you already pressed (a launch-time warm-up shows nothing on screen and says `loading the speech model…` in the menu instead), the same ember from the press until the mic's first audio lands, then lit — the start chime plays then too, so the lit lamp and the chime both mean the mic is hearing you, not that a key went down — hot when you speak, then a cool-out that collapses it to a hot dot and fades. **success is silent: the afterglow is the whole goodbye.** it copies what a gold-tinted Liquid Glass ribbon looks like in a key window. it is not Liquid Glass, because Liquid Glass draws dimmed — no refraction, no highlight — in any window that is not key, and the panel must never be key: it would take the keyboard from the app you are dictating into. the bare gold line it replaces (2026-08-12) vanished over light pages; the glass capsule with eleven bars before that is not coming back.
- **the pill.** the exceptional-message shape, and the only thing that ever speaks: `copied — secure field · ⌘V to paste` · `copied — focus changed · ⌘V to paste` · `copied — couldn't paste it · ⌘V to paste` · `copied — what you said before the lock · ⌘V to paste` · `the clipboard is busy — nothing was copied` · `couldn't transcribe — tap to try again` · `heard nothing` · `still finishing the last one` · `recording was lost` · `speech model failed — retrying` · `speech model isn't responding — restarting it` · `speech model didn't download — finish setup` · `downloading the speech model — about 460 mb` · `downloading the speech model — 42%` · `loading the speech model…` · `microphone access is off` · `no microphone available` · `microphone isn't responding` · `no sound from AirPods Pro` · `the mic changed — pasted what i had.` · `thirty seconds left` · `five minutes — that's the cap. pasted what i had.`. every pill is also posted as a VoiceOver announcement — the failure rule holds for users who cannot look. dark glass with pale gold text, legible over a page. it shares the tube's stage, so the tube fades into it rather than the window changing shape.
- **a pill with a button (ADR 0047).** a question gets one button, set into the pill's own glass: a small capsule of the same glass, its word in the pill's pale gold a step heavier, quiet, never system blue. three questions have one: `zoom call — record it?` with `record` for 15 s, `call ended — stop recording?` with `stop` for 30 s, and `still recording?` with `stop` for 30 s (§11). the button does what the menu item of the same name does, and nothing else on the hud starts or stops anything. a click on the pill beside the button is an answer too. its time stops while the pointer is over the pill, so it never leaves from under a hand on its way to the button. the panel takes the mouse only while the pointer is over such a pill, and it is never key, so a click on the button leaves the keyboard in the app you were typing in. a question waits its turn. it never goes up over a take, a sentence or setup, and pressing the dictation key takes the pill from it, unanswered. there is no key for the button; the menu offers the same actions, and that is the path for the keyboard and for VoiceOver. the button's accessibility label says the whole action (`record the zoom call`), and the question is announced like every pill. a pill without a button is unchanged.
- **the meeting's light (2026-10-03).** a full-screen call hides the menu bar and its badge, so while a meeting records the lamp keeps a light on: the same tube in the same place, lit and flat, quieter than any take and deaf to the voices in the room. it does not breathe or move for as long as the meeting runs, and it is drawn once, not sixty times a second. until the tap has heard the start sound it is the ember a take wears before its mic is heard. a pill takes the stage as always and the light is back when the pill leaves; a take allowed in those first moments has the lamp like any take. stop puts it out with the cool-out. two more looks are built and nothing shows them yet: getting ready, the ember, and a meeting with a problem, the same steady light in the attention colour. how bright it burns is one number, `HUDWaveMotion.meetingGlow`.
- **who sees it.** while a meeting is involved the panel is out of every screen capture: a share, a recording, a screenshot. that covers the meeting's light and every pill about a meeting — the reminder, both questions, `recording a meeting`, a gap, a problem the meeting records through, `writing it out…`, `saved`, and the failures. you see them; whoever is watching your share does not. dictation's lamp and pills stay in every capture, as they always were, so a screenshot of one still works in a bug report. a panel that went out of capture for a meeting stays out until it leaves the screen, so nothing on it flickers into a share mid-sentence. the menu bar badge cannot be hidden this way and shows in any share that includes the menu bar.

the rule: **a failed dictation must never look like a successful one.** anything that goes wrong cuts the afterglow short and says why. a mic that opens but sends no sound — nothing within a second (three for a bluetooth headset, which has to switch profile before it can be heard), or by key-up only zeros or under a tenth of a second — is a mic failure, not `heard nothing`: the pill names it as it names itself (`no sound from the microphone` when it won't), the next press opens a fresh one, and nothing is offered to retry, so pressing again records again. the input device changing mid-take ends the take but keeps it: what was heard up to the change is pasted, and then the pill says why the take ended without you, like the cap. sleep and the lock (the lid, ⌃⌘Q, a hot corner) end the take and keep it too (ADR 0044). it is written out and put on the clipboard, never pasted, because the field it was going to is behind the lock screen and a paste on the way back in could land in whatever you are typing by then. history keeps it if history is on. a take still being written out when the lock comes down is copied the same way, and one whose mic was still stopping when the mac slept is written out on wake. the pill waits until you unlock, or wake with no lock to get past, and so does any other pill said while the mac is away. while the mic is live the app holds the display awake (`dictating` in `pmset -g assertions`), so a long dictation, held or locked, doesn't sleep the screen under itself. it lets go the moment the mic is asked to stop.

no dock icon. menu-bar item: the brand badge → menu: fix a word…, try that again, undo learned: <word>, record a meeting, settings, finish setup / run onboarding again, update to <x>, about, copy diagnostics, quit. `update to <x>` appears only when the daily check heard of a newer version, and never during a meeting. it gets no dot on the badge, because an old version still works. `try that again` appears only in the two minutes after the speech model threw on a dictation — the samples are still in memory, and re-running them beats saying the whole paragraph over. `undo learned: <word>` appears only in the two minutes after the app learned a word from your fixes (§3). `copy diagnostics` puts the last fifty press records on the clipboard, then the last twenty meeting records (§11) — how each press or meeting ended, the mic, the timings, never a word of what was said — and says nothing: the clipboard is the answer. like copy timings, it waits for a paste and its clipboard restore to finish, so it never lands in the middle of one. a gold dot on the badge means the mic is live for a take; a meeting wears a gold rim instead (§11). a red dot on the badge means setup is wrong — a missing permission, or a speech model that never finished downloading. both leave the app unable to transcribe a word, so both wear the dot until they are fixed.

## 5. onboarding (once, one app click)

three fixed cards — what it is, the speech models, the permissions. the first has one consent action, "set up andrew dictate (~460 mb)", which starts the downloads; the last asks macOS. the dots jump, back goes back, nothing advances on its own. nothing downloads and hotkeys remain detection-only before that click, and no later launch starts a download for a job that was not ticked.

the click starts the parakeet v2 download and warmup and prompts for accessibility; the microphone is asked for from its row. one live checklist shows microphone, accessibility, and speech-model status; download progress stays inline, denied permissions link to system settings, and model failures can retry.

pre-roll and dictionary configuration are omitted. defaults apply: fn for dictation, pre-roll off. settings owns every option. the key is not configured here, it is *proved* here: screen two shows the bound key and asks for one press, and the key picker only appears once the shipped default has visibly failed to fire.

**onboarding forks by job (ADR 0040, 2026-08-29).** the first card carries two check rows: `dictation · ~460 mb` ticked, `meeting recording · ~2.9 gb` offered unticked. the button prices what is ticked; ticking meetings adds its rows and its download, unticking dictation removes them. meeting rows in the checklist: `meeting model`, and `system audio` — proved, not asked, by playing the start sound into the tap, which hears our own process like any other — that is what fires the real prompt (ADR 0021, one screen earlier). the click also makes the transcripts folder, which is what fires the ~/Documents prompt on the permissions screen instead of mid-save at the end of the first meeting; a folder it cannot make shows one more row, `transcripts folder`, and nothing appears when it can. someone who unticked meetings and later presses `record a meeting` gets this window back filtered to the meeting rows, headed `set up meeting recording`. **a meetings-only setup is never nagged for accessibility**: the gate reads which jobs were chosen before it reads the permissions, because there is no hotkey to be dead and system audio is proved at every capture anyway.

**the two sizes have three homes.** `OnboardingJobs.dictationDownload` / `.meetingsDownload` are the source; the README's install section quotes them, and so does the tap cask's `caveats` in jassuwu/homebrew-tap — a second repo no release automation rewrites, which is how it spent eight releases pricing the download at 450 MB. re-price a job and all three change, or `brew install` prices it wrong before the app ever opens.

when every row of every chosen job is ready the card says "ready", shows the key and the badge, and the button becomes "start dictating"; while a model is still coming down it stays "done"; when something is missing it says "close", because nothing is done. below the checklist, behind a divider so it reads as a coda and not a fourth permission, one ticked row: `start andrew dictate when you sign in` — registered by the last button and by nothing else, hidden when macOS has no registration to make. no account, no tour, no newsletter.

**permissions are re-verified, never remembered (2026-08-13).** there is no "skip" button (ADR 0029, 2026-08-22): someone who launched the app launched it in order to set it up, and macOS already provides the exit — the window is closable. **closing is the better exit**, because it records nothing and setup returns next launch; the old link marked onboarding dismissed and silenced it for good. only "done" claims setup finished. whether the app can dictate is asked of the system at launch, at reopen, on wake and unlock, and when macOS reports a trust change. a working setup is never nagged again; a broken one gets the window back at launch or reopen, and mid-session revocation only badges the menu bar — stealing focus while you type is the sin this app exists to prevent. settings shows what's missing and routes here, because this is the only place that knows how to ask.

## 6. settings (tabs — ADR 0034, 0038, 0040)

the native `Settings` scene, five glass tabs: **dictation** (setup banner only when something is missing · hotkey · pre-roll · sound feedback · the pipeline — heard → cleanup → pasted, one word each, a switch in each stage · the dictation model cards) · **dictionary** (wrong→right pairs, a `learned` mark and a one-click remove on rows the app added, import/export json) · **history** (`dictations | meetings`, keep-new-dictations toggle, delete) · **meetings** (the meeting model cards · save folder · how long meeting audio is kept · the hook — §11) · **general** (launch at login · the numbers dashboard · remove Andrew Dictate). the thing that opened you is where you live: nothing in settings opens another window except onboarding, which is the only surface that knows how to ask for a permission.

two rules, both learned the hard way: options that appear on more than one screen are **defined once** (`DictationOption`) and rendered twice — the copy cannot drift because there is only one copy. and the user-facing word for the asr backend is **speech model** everywhere; `engine` survives only in code (`ParakeetEngine`, `EngineVersion`). "parakeet 0.3" is a value, not a category.

## 7. instrumentation (internal)

every utterance logs its stage timestamps locally (debug menu to dump):

`keyDown → micFirstBuffer → keyUp → transcriptReady → cleaned → delivered`

working targets, not commitments: key-up → transcript ≤ 250ms, key-up → inserted ≤ 450ms (base M4, warm, p50). **the published quantity is key-up → inserted** — measured to the ⌘V keystroke reaching the target app, not to the clipboard restore that follows it; key-up → transcript would flatter us by excluding the span the claim is about (ADR 0025). "copy timings" ships in **release**: it prints p50/p95/max over delivered pastes only, with the sample size, the exclusions, and the machine/chip/os/engine/build it was measured on, above the per-utterance table it came from. anyone can produce that number on their own mac from their own speech, which is the point — it replaces the standalone bench harness rather than deferring it. **no competitor benchmark is published**: the only figure that exists anywhere is a rival founder's estimate, and a claim resting on that is not evidence.

## 8. distribution

- unsigned in v1 (ADR 0009): github releases dmg + personal tap cask `jassuwu/tap/andrew-dictate`; README documents the gatekeeper step honestly. signing + notarization + sparkle gate the "tell other people" milestone.
- **about screen:** FluidAudio (Apache-2.0) notice, parakeet weights (CC-BY-4.0) attribution, MIT license.
- **the update check (ADR 0043):** once a day, and when the menu opens on a check more than a day old, the app asks `dictate.jass.gg/api/latest?version=<running>`. the user agent and accept-language are pinned, so every copy sends the same request apart from the version. it waits out a dictation, a model load, or a meeting. offline doesn't count as the day's check; a reply without a version does. any failure shows nothing, and the automatic path never falls back to github. the endpoint (`apps/site/api/latest.ts`) reads github's `/releases/latest` redirect. its answer is `no-store`, so every check reaches it; only github's tag is cached, in memory for an hour per instance, and the last good tag is served while a lookup fails (ADR 0043, amended). for a brew install, clicking the line runs `brew upgrade --cask jassuwu/tap/andrew-dictate` in the background: the absolute brew path, no tty, a ten-minute limit. the line reads `updating…`, then `restart to finish`. a failure, a timeout, or an /Applications still holding the old version reads `couldn't update — command copied`, with the command on the clipboard and brew's last stderr line in the log. a click while dictating or recording does nothing. a dmg install, or a brew install with no brew at /opt/homebrew or /usr/local, gets the releases page. settings › general › `check for updates` turns it off, and off means no request.

## 10. open questions (parked, non-blocking)

- **branding — settled.** black + gold; the badge is the logo everywhere and is never a template image. art lives in `art/`: `icon-source.png` rasterizes to the icon set and the menu bar badge via `build.sh`, which then runs `og-compose.swift` for the og image and copies og/badge/favicon into `apps/site/public`. taglines: "escape the keyboard." + "free · open source · fully local".
- **website — shipped (2026-08-11):** dictate.jass.gg, astro, in `apps/site`.
- **HUD placement/personality — decided (2026-08-12):** bottom-centre, the lamp. see §4.
- ~~dictation history beyond "copy last" — deliberately absent~~ **superseded 2026-08-22 (ADR 0022).** two durable nouns: a **dictation** is your own speech and is kept until you delete it (raw + inserted text, 0600 on disk, no record of which app you were in); a **meeting recording** holds other people's words and is a separate type with its own retention rules, decided alongside meeting capture. the old test — "revisit only if losing a transcript hurts" — was measured and fails: nothing is lost, because a bad dictation gets fixed in place. history exists because a tool used a hundred times a day should leave a trace, not as a safety net.
- pre-roll buffer depth (~300ms is a starting guess) — tune once real first-word-loss data exists.
- per-channel language in a meeting — open (2026-09-12). translating *them* while leaving *you* as spoken needs the two sides decoded apart, which whisper now does, a stretch at a time, and two sides of large-v3 keep pace live (measured 2026-10: the decoder busy a third of the time in ordinary talk and six tenths in heavy overlap, the queue never growing). what is missing is the choice, not the speed: where it is set, and what it defaults to. until then the model decides for the whole room, and §11 says so.

## 11. meetings (ADR 0021, 0023, 0040, 0047, 0049)

a local meeting recorder. your mic is *you*; everything the mac plays is *them* — the call, whatever app it is in, and a video or a notification sound if one plays while it runs (ADR 0049). what comes out is **one english markdown transcript**, checked against the audio before the audio goes. the audio stays on this mac for a day by default, out of the transcripts folder, and is then deleted (ADR 0048).

- **start / stop:** `record a meeting` in the menu, one item: there is no app to pick, because the tap hears the whole mac. manual both ends: the app watches which apps use the mic so it can ask, and it never acts on what it sees. nothing starts or stops a recording but you. the lamp says `recording a meeting`, then keeps its low, steady light until you stop (§4); while recording the menu reads `recording · 12:34`, offers `stop recording` and `live transcript`, and hides `fix a word…` — dictation is refused mid-meeting and mid-rebuild, and the hud says why. the menu-bar badge wears a gold rim round its edge — the mic is live, and it is a meeting, not a take.
- **record with:** under `record a meeting`, and only where it is, `record with ▸` lists the other meeting models on this mac by name and trait, and is there only when there is one; choosing one starts a meeting named as the menu item would name it and heard by that model for that meeting alone — the file's `engine`, the spool and a recovery all say which — while the default in settings, the next `record a meeting`, the reminder's `record` and the hotkey keep using the default.
- **a hotkey:** unset until you set one, in settings › meetings, where you click the chip and press a combination with ⌃, ⌥ or ⌘ in it; a press then does what the menu item does — starts a meeting, sends a mac with no meeting model to setup, or stops the one that records, and a meeting still writing its file is not recording, so a press starts the next — and it is registered with the system as a hot key, which asks for no accessibility, so it never touches the dictation key's monitor; a combination that holds the dictation key's modifier, or is on esc, is refused on the row with a few words why, and so is a dictation key that the combination holds.
- **proving it can hear:** capture starts by playing the start sound; the tap must hear it (ADR 0021). never heard → `can't hear the mac — opening setup`, which opens setup at privacy › system audio recording. went silent after working → rebuild; the gap is kept with its timestamps and the file says `complete: false`. silence while nothing else on the mac is playing is a quiet room, not a dead tap, and is left alone — the HAL is asked whether anything but this app is playing once a second, on the tap's own queue, never from the main thread. a tap that stops calling back at all — sleep, a locked screen, a dead driver — is a gap like any other, found by the wall clock rather than by audio time, which stops with it. the tap has eight seconds to open and five to close: one that never opens is `can't hear`, and one that never closes is left behind while the file is written anyway.
- **the mic:** whatever the mac says the default input is when you press record, or the built-in mic if that is none a meeting can use. it follows the default input from then on: airpods connecting, a usb mic plugged in, a pick in control centre, or the mic going away (airpods dying) moves the meeting to the new default, or to the built-in mic when there is none. the move waits for the hardware to settle, a second and a half after the last change and three at most after the first, and is one move however many changes came. a new tap and mic come up beside the old ones, which keep recording until the new pair delivers; there is no start sound, nothing on the lamp, and the meeting's clock carries on: no hole while the old mic is still recording, and only the seconds nothing was heard when it has gone. a new mic that never delivers is given five seconds and dropped, and the meeting stays where it was, or, if its mic has gone, tries the built-in one. a mic with several inputs is heard on the ones carrying sound; an aggregate input (audio midi setup) is heard through the mics inside it.
- **the reminder (ADR 0047):** the app watches one thing, which processes hold the mic and which play audio, read from Core Audio's process objects. it needs no new permission and reads no window title, tab address or calendar. a listener on the default input says when anyone takes the mic, and only then, while a meeting records, or while a call is on, is the process list read: every two seconds, or every ten while the mic is held by this app alone (pre-roll keeps it open, a dictation takes it). a call is still read after its app lets go of the mic, because a call app that closes the mic on mute is still playing everyone else, and that call goes on. nobody on a call costs a listener and nothing else, and a mac with no meeting model does not even listen. the call apps are zoom, teams, slack, facetime and the browsers, a helper process counting for its app. this app's own process never counts, so dictation and a meeting's own capture are never a call. one call app holding the mic and playing audio for three seconds is a call. with nothing recording, the pill asks `zoom call — record it?` with a `record` button for 15 seconds, once per call. `record` starts a meeting exactly as the menu does, named after the call, so the file, its front matter and the hook say `zoom`. a click beside the button is a no for that call. after that the menu leads with `zoom call · not recording` above `record a meeting` until the call ends, and the menu bar icon shows it. `record a meeting` from the menu while a call is on is named after the call too. a browser tab using the mic for something that is not a call will be asked about; the cost is a pill that leaves after 15 seconds. the 15 and 30 second values are guesses until real calls tune them.
- **the call ending:** while a recording runs, the call app doing neither for 30 seconds asks once, `call ended — stop recording?`, with `stop`, for 30 seconds. no answer leaves the recording running. nothing stops by itself, and that call is not asked about again. a meeting started with no call never gets this question; it still gets the nudge.
- **the nudge:** an hour of silence asks `still recording?` twice: through a notification with `keep going` / `stop`, and on the pill with `stop` for 30 seconds, so it reaches someone who has notifications off. a click on the pill beside the button is `keep going`. the first answer, from either, is the one acted on, and the other is withdrawn. it asks; it never acts.
- **the lamp's other lines**, each for a failure that must not look like success (§4): `nothing was heard, nothing kept` (stopped before the probe was heard), `meeting model failed — …` (the engine would not load; capture ends, the spool stays for recovery), `couldn't save the transcript — … kept for next launch`. `can't hear` ends the session too — the menu never says `recording` over a tap that is not delivering. dictation mid-sentence when you press record gets `finish dictating first`; the mic is one.
- **engine:** whisper large-v3 (full by default — turbo is the smaller card, and turbo cannot translate) via WhisperKit, **or parakeet v3**, the model dictation already offers, via FluidAudio: the fastest card, with nothing to download for anyone who dictates with v3, and it knows english and the european languages only — hindi, or anything else, comes out as nonsense. every model hears the two channels apart: silero, FluidAudio's voice model, which ships inside the app, finds where speech begins and ends on each, and each stretch of speech is decoded once — at most 25 s for whisper and 15 s for parakeet, and talk that runs longer is cut at a quiet moment just before that. a stretch is a line in the live view and a turn in the file. a `you` stretch that is only the far side coming back through the mic on the mac's own speakers — its loudness rising and falling as theirs did a moment before, while they were talking through most of it — is let go before it is decoded, so they are said once, as `them`; when it isn't clear the stretch is kept, because a doubled line costs less than a lost one. the language is the model's choice and not the speaker's: large-v3 translates every stretch into english, your own hindi included; turbo cannot translate, so it writes every language as spoken. speakers are labelled per channel; language is not. the meeting loads a model of its own and lets it go when it ends; dictation's parakeet is never borrowed. **the live pass is the transcript.** FluidAudio's diarizer splits `them` into `them 1`, `them 2`… as the meeting goes: it hears the far side a minute at a time while the meeting records, so stop has only the last minute left for it. after stop the file is written, the spool deleted — seconds, not a batch re-pass, however long the meeting ran. the speakers are numbered 1, 2, 3 in the order they first speak in the file, none skipped; a stretch the diarizer could not hear leaves its lines plain `them`. a spool orphaned by a crash is transcribed at next launch and saved flagged `recovered`, and recovery never deletes a meeting. audio it cannot read, and a folder with audio whose manifest will not decode, are set aside in the spool's `unreadable` folder, not deleted — only audio that reads and has no samples in it is let go, because nothing was captured. a spool whose model is gone is read with another that is installed, whisper large then turbo then parakeet, and the file's `engine` names the one that wrote it; with none installed the spool waits for a launch that has one, and no attempt is counted. two failed launches set a spool aside too. settings › history says how many are set aside and offers `show in finder` and `try again`: each recording comes back to the spool with its tries cleared and is read once more, one that fails again goes straight back aside, and a folder that has no manifest is given one — `meeting`, the audio's own date, the default model.
- **live view:** a floating glass panel, open for your first meeting and remembered after that; toggled from the menu; interleaved `you` / `them` lines with timestamps, each a finished stretch as it is decoded. nothing on it is dimmed or rewritten. it is out of every screen capture, always: it holds other people's words, and a share of your screen does not show it.
- **who sees what:** you see the lamp's light, the meeting's pills and the live view; a screen share, a recording or a screenshot sees none of them (§4). a share that includes the menu bar still shows the badge's gold rim, and macOS's own microphone indicator beside it. measured on macOS 27 against `screencapture`, ScreenCaptureKit's screenshots and live streams, and single-window capture; a share from zoom itself is still to be checked.
- **the file:** `<folder you picked>/meetings/2026-08/2026-08-29-1402-meeting.md`, default parent `~/andrew-dictate/`, no spaces — deliberately not `~/Documents` or `~/Desktop`, which icloud syncs; a folder you pick that does sync says so on the row. front matter: app (`meeting`, or the call app's name when a call was on at the start: `zoom`), started, ended, duration, engine, speakers (in order of first appearance, the labels the body uses), words, complete, reason (only when incomplete: `audio was lost in 2 gaps` unless something else says why), gaps, recovered. the body is one paragraph per speaker turn, `[hh:mm:ss] you: …`: a speaker's consecutive lines are one paragraph stamped with the first, and a monologue gets a new paragraph a minute after the last one began, so there is always a time to find your place by; talk that picks up again after a silence is a new paragraph too. `them 1` and `them 2` are different speakers and never merge. an incomplete transcript also says in its body where the holes are, because nobody reads front matter. files from before this layout (no `ended`, `speakers` or `words`, a line per fragment) are still read as they are. `meetings/README.md` sits beside the month folders and tells an agent what the files are and what each key means: written once, when a transcript is saved and none is there, never rewritten or overwritten, never a row in history. kept until you delete it — in finder or in history, where the row opens it: `open`, or a double-click, hands the markdown to whatever editor you already keep. there is no reader of ours — `live transcript` exists only while the meeting is running. the file is 0600 and the two folders the app makes for it 0700; the folder you picked is left as it is. **the saved moment:** the lamp says `saved · 1h 42m`, a notification names the file and offers `show in finder` — the pill cannot be clicked and is gone before you are back at the mac — and for ten minutes the menu leads with `show last meeting in finder`. a write that failed says so and offers no button, because there is no file yet.
- **hook:** settings → meetings → *after a meeting is saved, run:* one executable chosen by file picker. invoked detached once the file is closed: transcript path as `$1`, json on stdin (`event`, `transcript`, `folder`, `app`, `started_at`, `duration_s`, `complete`, `gaps`, `recovered`, `again`), the same as `ANDREW_*` env. `again` is `true` (`ANDREW_AGAIN=1`) when the file was replaced because its meeting was transcribed again from its kept audio, and the hook runs a second time for it so an agent's copy does not go stale; on a first save it is `false` (`ANDREW_AGAIN=0`). no timeout; output to `hooks.log`. a failure is announced once on the hud and stays on the row as `last run: … · exit 3 · open log`; a success stays as `last run: … · ok`, so you can tell a hook that ran from one that never fired. the only event is `meeting-saved`.
- **the record:** every meeting leaves exactly one record of how it ended, whatever the ending, the way a press does (§4): saved, saved after a recovery, nothing kept (the tap never heard, or you stopped first), the model failed (audio kept), the transcript could not be written (audio kept), a recovery that failed and was counted or set aside, a spool it could not read and set aside, a spool waiting for a model, a spool with nothing in it. it holds the app, the model, when it started and how long it ran, the gaps and the seconds they took, turns and words per side, the seconds from stop to the file, what the tap went through by the meeting's own clock (a gap began, a gap ended, a rebuild failed, the mic changed, a move to another mic failed, the meeting fell back to the built-in mic), and an engine's decode counts when it keeps them. never a word of what was said, and no path to the file. one notice-level line each in the unified log, and the last two hundred in `meeting-records.jsonl` beside `presses.jsonl`: 0600, taken with your dictations when you remove the app's data, and a record written before a field existed still reads. `copy diagnostics` hands over the last twenty.
- **models:** each job picks its own model from the same cards; the card states the consequence (whisper for dictation is slow; parakeet for meetings has no hindi). the meeting model downloads at onboarding when the job is ticked, or the first time you press record, and what a meeting reads besides it comes down with it — whisper's tokenizer, and the speaker-split models — so no meeting waits on the network, at its start or at its end. silero is in the app (1 mb, MIT) and is never fetched; if it will not load, a meeting tells speech by loudness. a mac that was set up before these came down with the model is the one exception: its first meeting fetches whichever is missing, once, the way setup would have, and only says `meeting model failed` if the tokenizer still cannot be read. a meeting whose speaker-split models are not there yet leaves `them` unsplit.
- **the coverage check (ADR 0048):** at stop, before any audio is deleted, what was transcribed is held against what was said. the stretch transcriber knows, per side, the seconds of speech it cut (bleed let go is not counted) and how much of that the engine read; the spool says on its own, a block at a time, how long the far side was louder than the silence floor. a side with 10 s of speech or more and over a fifth of it unread is thin (`some of what was said could not be read`); a side with 30 s read or more and under half a word a second of it is thin (`far fewer words than the talk that was heard`); a minute of the far side in the spool with no speech cut from it is thin (`the other side was heard and nothing of it was read`) — and for an engine that keeps no count, which is judged on words alone, no words of theirs. the numbers are provisional, in one place, and every result goes in the record. thin → the lamp says `the transcript looked thin — reading the audio again…`, a fresh transcriber for the same model reads the whole spool, and that reading is checked on its own numbers: passing, it is the transcript; still thin, or thrown, the reading with more words is written with `complete: false` and the reason, and that meeting's audio is kept until you delete it. either way the file is written once and never changed after. a meeting nobody spoke in passes, and its body says `> nobody spoke`. a recovered spool is checked and not read again — it already was.
- **kept audio (ADR 0048):** once the file is written, the spool is compressed — AAC in an `.m4a`, 16 kHz, `you` and `them` still two channels, written a block at a time — into `meeting-audio/` in application support, 0600 inside 0700, never the transcripts folder, and the spool goes. beside it a small json says which transcript it belongs to, when the meeting started, the model, and until when it is kept, or that it is kept until you delete it. a spool that will not compress is kept as it was, `.caf`. settings › meetings › *keep meeting audio*: `delete at once`, `one day` (the default) or `seven days`, read when a meeting starts. `delete at once` deletes it the moment the file is written, as before. a thin transcript's audio is kept until you delete it, whatever the setting. past its date it is deleted without asking: at launch — one look at one folder, nothing built for meetings — after every meeting, and once a day while the app runs. a meeting whose audio exists says so on its history row, `audio until fri 14:02` or `audio kept`, which offers `delete audio now`; deleting the transcript there deletes its audio. while the audio is kept the row also offers `transcribe again with ▸` and every meeting model that is installed, the one that wrote the transcript included, since the same model again is a fair retry. it is off while a meeting is being recorded or another is being transcribed again, and the row says which (`recording`, `one at a time`); the row shows `transcribing again…` while it runs, and the action is not there once the audio is gone. the kept audio is read whole, as a recovery's spool is, by a fresh transcriber for the model you picked, then goes through the coverage check and the speaker split as at the stop, and the new reading replaces the transcript file in place — atomically, at the same path. it is the one time a file changes after it is written, and it changes because you asked: `engine`, `speakers`, `words`, `complete`, `reason` and the body are the new reading's; `app`, `started`, `ended`, `duration_s`, `gaps` and `recovered` stay as the file says (so the check's "written once and never changed after" holds for the stop, and a rerun is its one exception). if the model throws or will not load, the audio is gone or empty, or the new reading is thin where the file was whole or finds nobody speaking where the file has words, the file is left exactly as it was, the lamp says `couldn't transcribe again — …`, and the audio stays. a thin file whose new reading passes is an ordinary meeting's now: its audio is kept as long as the setting says, counted from then; every other audio date stays where it was, and a rerun never deletes audio. the lamp says `transcribing again with whisper large…`, then `transcribed again · whisper large`; the hook runs again with `again: true`; and the meeting record gets one record per rerun, marked `again=1`, with the model, the coverage result, turns and words per side and the seconds it took — its outcome `unchanged` when the file was left as it was. the app quitting while it keeps the audio loses nothing: the next launch finishes the job and never writes the meeting out twice. removing the app's data takes it with the spool. an hour of it is about 25 mb, and takes about four seconds to compress.
- **silence is not damage:** a quiet far side is never rebuilt, gapped or chirped at for being quiet, however long it lasts — presenting to a muted room sounds exactly like a dead tap. only when it has been silent for two minutes while something on the mac is playing is the tap asked again, the way it was at the start: a quiet tone of ours, a third of a second of 1 khz at -40 dbfs, which it hears if it hears anything. heard, nothing happens and nothing is said, and the next question waits another two minutes; not heard, the tap is dead. whether anything but this app is playing is asked of the HAL once a second, on the tap's own queue, never from the main thread, and no answer counts as nothing playing. a tone that cannot be played at all is no evidence either way. a tap that stops calling back — sleep, a locked screen, a dead driver — is dead too, found by the wall clock rather than by audio time, which stops with it.
- **a dead tap:** a gap, kept with its timestamps, and the file says `complete: false`. the tap is rebuilt once the hardware has had a second and a half to settle, tried three times in a row two then five seconds apart, and trusted again only once it hears its own start sound. three tries and still nothing is a **problem**, not the end: the lamp says `can't hear the call — still recording your side`, your side goes on being recorded, the gap stays open, and the tap is tried every thirty seconds until one works — `hearing the call again` — or the meeting is stopped, and its gap runs to the end of the file. your side is recorded by the mic alone: the first rebuild that fails brings the mic up on its own, in an aggregate with no tap, through the same chunks with the far side as silence and on the meeting's one clock, and it follows the default input like the whole rig does. each later try builds the whole rig beside it, and the mic alone goes once the tap is back. if not even the mic alone will come up, nothing is being recorded, and the lamp says `can't hear the call or your mic — still trying` instead. no problem during a call opens a window or takes focus. the mac is kept from idle sleep while a meeting records; the display may sleep. every number here is a guess until real meetings tune it.
- **problems that stay:** several can stand at once, one of each kind, the worst first: the call unheard, your mic unheard, the audio not saved, the disk nearly full. each is said on the lamp when it begins and again when it clears, each clears on its own, and none opens a window. **your mic:** the mic handing over nothing but silence — under 0.0001 rms, far below the hiss of any real room — for ten seconds while the far side talks is `can't hear your mic — macbook pro microphone`, named as the mac names it, until the mic is heard again (`hearing your mic again`). both sides silent is a quiet room and says nothing. **a muted mic:** the mic's own mute switch on, or its input volume at nothing — read from the mic every two seconds, on the tap's own queue — is silent on purpose and not a fault: nothing is held against it while it lasts, a mic problem standing is over, and the lamp says `your mic is muted`, once the meeting is recording if it was muted before. **the audio:** a chunk the spool will not take is `can't save the audio — still transcribing`: the live transcript goes on, every chunk refused is counted, and the first it takes again ends it (`saving the audio again`). **the disk:** under a gigabyte free on the disk the spool is on, when the meeting starts or at a look once a minute after, is `disk nearly full`; the meeting records all the same, and a later look that finds room ends it (`the disk has room again`).
- **a start that fails** says which part failed and points at that part's fix. the mic permission is asked of the mac before anything is built or loaded, every meeting, so a grant taken back is caught for a setup that was meetings only too: not allowed → `the mic isn't allowed — opening setup`, and setup opens at its permissions. no mic at all, or one that would not start or did not within the eight seconds → `can't start your mic — yeti`, or `no mic to record you with`, and no window: nothing in setup starts a mic. the tap → `can't hear the mac — opening setup`, as before, and so does a failure that does not say which part it was. none of them keeps anything, and each leaves its own reason in the record.
